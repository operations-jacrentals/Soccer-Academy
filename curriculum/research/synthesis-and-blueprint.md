# Synthesis & Blueprint

What the three models agree on, and what we duplicate into Soccer-Academy — framed
for our plan: **cover every index building block, age-by-age, 8 → 13, in order.**

## The shared DNA (our non-negotiables)

> The full intersection derivation — comparing all three dimension-by-dimension and
> merging only what they have in common — is in **[`merged-model.md`](merged-model.md)**.
> The short version:

Everything the three converge on becomes a founding principle for us:

1. **One coherent philosophy of play, taught identically at every age.**
2. **Technique + game intelligence before physicality** — "train brains before bodies."
3. **Rondos, positional games, small-sided games, and maximum ball touches.**
4. **Development over winning** — results are a by-product.
5. **The whole player** — technical, tactical, physical, psychological, + education/character.
6. **Long-term and individualized** — each child on their own trajectory.

## Why these three fit a homeschool academy especially well

- **Ajax's Montessori individualization** *is* homeschooling — one-on-one, self-paced, mastery-based. We're already set up for it.
- **Development-over-winning** is free for us: no league table, no pressure to win — exactly the environment all three say they *wish* they had more of.
- **Rondos / small-sided games** need tiny numbers and space — ideal for a micro-group.
- **Barça Innovation Hub + Ajax's public manuals** give us an open-source methodology library, so we're not inventing from scratch.
- All three explicitly pair football with **education** — our "soccer + smarts" mission is their model, not a bolt-on.

## The blueprint: how this drives the age-by-age curriculum

**Two inputs, one output.** The [index](../index/README.md) is the *checklist of building blocks*; these models are the *staging + teaching method*. Each age year we:

1. **Pick the building blocks** from the index appropriate to that age (filter by difficulty tier + LTAD stage).
2. **Sequence them** foundational → advanced, in order, 8 → 13.
3. **Balance the four corners** every block (Technical / Physical / Psychological / Social).
4. **Teach them the academy way** — one game model, rondos & SSGs, max touches, development-first.
5. **Assess** with a simple TIPS/four-corner rubric each term.

### Our 8–13 window on the development map

Ages 8–13 sit in the **"golden age of learning"** (`L2T`, ~9–12) plus its edges —
the window all three academies treat as the technical-foundation years. Mapping to
**Ajax's stages** gives us a ready spine:

| Age | LTAD | Ajax stage | Primary emphasis (technique-first throughout) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8** | FUN→L2T | 2 | ball mastery, first touch, **creativity & 1v1** ("creators, not tacticians"), fun; movement ABCs |
| **9** | L2T | 2–3 | technical range on both feet, dribbling & moves, intro rondos (3v1/4v1), scanning |
| **10** | L2T | 3 | first touch under light pressure, passing combinations, positional rondos, small-sided decisions |
| **11** | L2T | 3 | 1v1 mastery, finishing, receiving on the half-turn, positional-play basics (zones/space) |
| **12** | L2T→T2T | 3–4 | game intelligence, positional play, roles begin, pressing/defending principles |
| **13** | T2T | 4 | tactical awareness in the game model, position sense, athletic development ramps (through PHV) |

> Physicality is **never the selector** in this window — it develops through games
> and movement skill, per all three models. Character, focus, and decision-making
> (the "smarts") are trained explicitly alongside the ball every year.

### What we commit to (founding rules for the program)

- [ ] Choose and document **one game model** (recommended: possession + positional play, à la Barça/Ajax).
- [ ] **Rondo + small-sided game every session**; maximize touches; minimize lines/queues.
- [ ] **Development over winning**, written into the charter.
- [ ] **Individual development plan** per child (Montessori-style), reviewed each term.
- [ ] **Four-corner + TIPS** assessment rubric, run termly.
- [ ] Every session carries a **"smarts" thread** — decision-making, character, or an academic tie-in.
