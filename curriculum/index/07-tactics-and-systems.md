# 07 · Tactics & Systems

How eleven players are organized and coordinated: formations, the phases and
principles of play, and the attacking/defending concepts that make a team more
than the sum of its parts.

---

## The four moments (phases of play)

Every second of a match is one of four moments. A game model defines behavior in each.

1. **In possession (attacking organization)** — building, progressing, creating, scoring.
2. **Out of possession (defensive organization)** — pressing/containing, protecting the goal.
3. **Attacking transition** — the moment you *win* the ball (counter or secure).
4. **Defensive transition** — the moment you *lose* the ball (counter-press or recover shape).

## Principles of play

**Attacking:** penetration · support (depth/angles) · width · mobility · improvisation/creativity.
**Defending:** delay (pressure) · cover (support) · balance · compactness (concentration) · control & restraint (discipline).

These are the timeless "grammar" — teach them before any specific system.

---

## Formations (systems)

Each is a starting shape and a set of trade-offs; teams morph between shapes in different phases.

- **4-4-2 (flat)** — simple, balanced, two banks of four; classic partnerships. Trade-off: can be outnumbered centrally.
- **4-4-2 diamond** — narrow midfield diamond, a #10 and a #6; strong centrally, needs full-backs for width.
- **4-3-3** — winger-led attack, midfield trio (often 1 pivot + 2 eights); pressing-friendly.
- **4-2-3-1** — double pivot + a #10 behind a lone striker; very balanced, popular modern default.
- **4-1-4-1** — a single pivot shields; compact mid-block out of possession.
- **4-3-2-1 ("Christmas tree")** — narrow, two tucked-in tens; concedes width.
- **3-5-2 / 3-4-1-2** — back three + wing-backs; central overloads, width from WBs.
- **3-4-3 / 3-4-2-1** — back three with attacking width and front-three threat.
- **5-3-2 / 5-4-1** — back five; defensive solidity, counter-attacking base.
- **4-4-1-1 / 4-5-1** — compact, pragmatic, counter-oriented.
- *Historic:* **2-3-5 (pyramid)**, **WM**, **catenaccio**, **Total Football** — useful for teaching how ideas evolved.

**System literacy:** formations describe *shape*, not *style*. A 4-3-3 can press high or sit deep. Teach players to read numerical match-ups (e.g., their front three vs. our back four).

## Team identity & game model

- **Game model** — the club's agreed way of playing across all four moments, taught identically at every age so promotion between teams is seamless. The reference every session points back to.
- **Playing identity / "DNA"** — the recognizable style a club commits to (e.g., proactive, possession-based, positional).
- **The three Ps (Barcelona)** — **P**ossession · **P**osition · **P**ressing — a compact statement of a positional-play identity.
- **Recognizable principles (Ajax)** — attractive, offensive, creative, fast, fair, and preferably played in the opponent's half.

## Attacking concepts

- **Building from the back (playing out)** — beating the first line of press with the GK + defenders.
- **Positional play (juego de posición)** — occupy zones/half-spaces to create superiorities: **numerical** (more players), **positional** (better location), **qualitative** (better match-up). Zones, lines, and the half-spaces are the map.
  - **The 5-lane grid** — the pitch split into **five vertical lanes** (two wings · two **half-spaces** · one central) crossed by horizontal lines. Coaching guidelines: keep players out of the *same* lane, hold ~10–15 m spacing to form triangles/diamonds, and don't stack a lane. Popularized by Cruyff/Guardiola.
  - **Half-space (Halbraum)** — the lane between wing and center: close enough to goal to shoot/thread a pass, wide enough to escape the crowded middle. Prime zone for inverted wingers, mezzalas, and overlapping runs.
- **Possession play** — controlling the game by keeping the ball, moving the opponent.
- **Direct play** — fast forward progression, bypassing midfield when it's on.
- **Counter-attack** — exploiting a disorganized defense immediately after winning the ball.
- **Overloads** — creating 2v1 / 3v2 in an area to break through, then exploiting the free man elsewhere ("overload to isolate").
- **Third-man runs & combinations** — A→B→C, where C arrives unmarked.
- **Give-and-go (1-2) / wall pass** — the basic combination to beat a defender.
- **La pausa** — the deliberate *pause* on the ball to let a better option develop (draw a defender, wait for the late run) instead of rushing the pass. Composure as a weapon.
- **Overlap & underlap** — supporting runs outside/inside the ball-carrier.
- **Switching play** — moving the ball to the underloaded far side.
- **Width & depth** — stretching the defense horizontally and vertically to open gaps.
- **Rotations & movement patterns** — coordinated position swaps to disorganize markers.
- **Isolation (1v1 iso)** — deliberately matching your best dribbler against a lone defender.
- **Cut-backs & attacking the box** — pulling the ball back to arriving runners; near/far-post and penalty-spot runs.
- **Zone 14** — the central pocket just outside the penalty area (the "golden square" between the half-spaces); match-analysis research flags it as one of the highest goal-assist-value areas to receive and create from.
- **Rest attack / staying connected** — keeping enough support so you can sustain pressure.

## Defending concepts

- **Pressing schemes** — **high press** (win it high), **mid-block** (invite then trap), **low-block** (defend deep, protect the box).
- **Pressing triggers** — cues to spring the press (a bad touch, a back-pass, a sideways pass, a specific receiver).
- **Gegenpressing / counter-press** — winning the ball back *immediately* after losing it, before the opponent settles.
- **Defensive line height** — high (compress space, risk balls in behind) vs. deep (safe, cedes territory).
- **Offside trap** — stepping up in unison to catch attackers offside.
- **Zonal vs. man-marking (and hybrids)** — defend space vs. defend a player; most teams blend both.
- **Compactness** — horizontal and vertical tightness; "shrinking the pitch" when out of possession.
- **Showing inside/outside** — angling pressure to force the opponent where you want them.
- **Cover shadows** — using body position to block a passing lane while pressing.
- **Screening & tracking** — protecting the space in front of the back line; following runners.
- **Rest defense** — the structure kept *while attacking* to stop the counter.
- **Defending the box** — set positions, second balls, blocking crosses and shots.

## Set-piece tactics

- **Attacking corners** — inswing/outswing, near-post flick-ons, screens/blocks, short corners, targeting zones.
- **Defending corners** — zonal, man, or hybrid; guarding posts; clearing + counter.
- **Free kicks** — direct routines, wall management, indirect combinations.
- **Throw-ins** — patterns, quick throws, the long throw as a weapon.
- **Penalties** — placement strategy, keeper research (see [05](05-psychology-and-mental.md)).

> **Curriculum note:** Introduce tactics through the **principles** and **small-
> sided games** (a 4v4 already contains width, depth, support, and pressing) long
> before formations. Systems and role detail come in `T2T`+; principles and
> game-sense come much earlier through guided questions ("where's the space?").
