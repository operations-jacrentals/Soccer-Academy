# 03 · Training Methodology & Drills

*How* players are trained: the practice philosophies, session structures, and a
catalog of drill types by theme. Method matters as much as content — the same
skill taught two ways produces very different learners.

---

## Practice philosophies & training styles

- **Isolated / technical practice** — repeat a technique with no defender (grooving mechanics). High reps, low realism. Best for first exposure. `FUN`
- **Unopposed → opposed progression** — add pressure gradually (passive → active → full defender). The core progression logic.
- **Small-sided games (SSG)** — 3v3–7v7; more touches, decisions, and involvement per player than 11v11. The workhorse of youth development.
- **Rondos** — keep-away in a circle/grid (4v1, 5v2, 6v3). Teaches quick passing, body shape, pressing, and support angles. A daily staple.
- **Possession games** — positional keep-ball (e.g., 7v7 + neutrals) training retention and shape.
- **Positional play / juego de posición grids** — zoned games teaching where to be and superiorities (see [07](07-tactics-and-systems.md)).
- **Phase of play** — rehearsing one phase (e.g., build-up) directionally toward a goal, often overloaded.
- **Functional / position-specific training** — reps in a player's real zone and role.
- **Pattern play / shadow play** — rehearsing movement patterns, often unopposed, to build automatisms.
- **Wave / transition games** — repeated attack-vs-defense waves; trains the moment of turnover.
- **Game-based approach (TGfU — Teaching Games for Understanding)** — learn through modified games that pose tactical problems; technique serves the game.
- **Play–Practice–Play (US Soccer grassroots model)** — a *player-centered* session shape: open with player-led small-sided **play**, move to a targeted **practice** activity (SSG/rondo/drill), then return to free **play**. Game-first and fun-first; the flow the "Session structure" below follows.
- **Structured training (Paco Seirul·lo, FC Barcelona)** — a complex-/dynamic-systems method: develop the whole player through **preferential simulation situations** — global, game-like tasks that load the systems the game actually demands — rather than isolated exercises. Kin to the constraints-led idea below.
- **Constraints-Led Approach (CLA) / ecological dynamics** — manipulate task/environment/individual constraints so solutions *emerge* (guided by the STEP model below). Less telling, more designing.
- **Differential learning** — deliberately varied, "noisy" reps (never the same twice) to build adaptable movement.
- **Deliberate practice vs. deliberate play** — focused, effortful, feedback-rich work vs. free, playful, child-led games. Youth need **both**.
- **Block vs. random (variable) practice** — massed reps of one skill vs. interleaving several. Block feels better short-term; random builds better retention/transfer (contextual interference).
- **Whole–part–whole** — play the whole game, isolate a part to fix, return to the whole.
- **Overload / underload games** — numerical advantage to bias learning (e.g., 4v2 to train the attack; 2v4 to train defending).

## Session structure

- **Arrival / free-play activity** — ball each, touches from the first minute.
- **RAMP warm-up** — Raise, Activate, Mobilize, Potentiate (replaces static stretching).
- **Technical block** — the day's core skill, low-to-high pressure.
- **Skill / SSG block** — apply the skill in a game-realistic, opposed setting.
- **Conditioned / phase game** — bigger game with a rule that biases the theme.
- **Free game (scrimmage)** — the whole game; let them play.
- **Cool-down / reflection** — light movement + a question or two ("what did you notice?").

**STEP model** (how to adjust any activity): **S**pace · **T**ask · **E**quipment · **P**eople.
**Coaching interventions:** *freeze* (stop-and-show), *in-flow* (cue on the move), *on the run*, *out-of-flow* (individual chat), *guided discovery* via questions.

## Periodization & load

- **Tactical periodization (Vítor Frade)** — plan the week (the **morphocycle**) around the **game model**, treating technical/tactical/physical/psychological as one inseparable whole (*specificity* is the supra-principle). Acquisition days rotate an emphasis — **tension** (strength) · **duration** (endurance) · **velocity** (speed) — each paired with the matching tactical principles/sub-principles, bracketed by recovery and activation days.
- **Block periodization** — concentrated blocks targeting one quality.
- **Undulating / non-linear** — varying intensity day to day.
- **Load management** — training load (see [09](09-metrics-and-statistics.md): RPE, ACWR), recovery, and — for youth — respecting growth/maturation.
- **Peaking & tapering** — reducing load to arrive fresh for key matches.

## Drill catalog by theme

Concise, reusable drill *types*. Expand into full session plans at curriculum time.

**Passing & receiving**
- Rondos (4v1, 5v2) · passing gates (score by passing through cones) · Y-drill / third-man combinations · triangle & diamond passing · positional grids · one-touch circuits · switch-play (big grid, two goals wide).

**First touch & control**
- Wall/rebounder touches · triangle receive-and-move · receiving on the turn · aerial control from a feed · "in the mixer" (control under a passive presser).

**Dribbling & 1v1**
- Cone slaloms / weaves · 1v1 to gates · 1v1 to goal (attacker vs. defender) · "king of the ring" (protect your ball, knock out others) · sharks & minnows · dribble-relay races · move-of-the-week station.

**Finishing & shooting**
- Rapid-fire finishing · combination-to-finish (1-2 then shoot) · crossing-and-finishing · 2v2 / 3v3 to goal · 1v1 vs. keeper · rebound/poacher reactions · shooting from the edge after a turn · finishing under fatigue.

**Defending**
- 1v1 defending (jockey & win) · 2v1 & 2v2 (press-cover) · pressing traps / triggers · defending the cross · recovery-run races · back-four shape & shifting (shadow).

**Transition**
- 4v4 (+2) transition games · counter-attack waves · "score-then-defend" · numbers-up-numbers-down.

**Possession & shape**
- 7v7 + neutrals keep-ball · positional games in zones · build-up vs. a press (GK + back line + mids vs. forwards).

**Set pieces**
- Rehearsed corner/free-kick routines · defensive set-piece organization · throw-in patterns.

**Physical / athletic** (see [04](04-attributes-and-physical.md))
- SAQ (speed-agility-quickness) ladders & hurdles · plyometric circuits · interval / fartlek runs · repeated-sprint sets · small-sided games *as* conditioning · movement-skill (ABC) games · mobility flows.

**Goalkeeping**
- Handling & set-position reps · footwork + shot-stopping · low/high diving · crosses & claiming · distribution (throw/kick targets) · playing-out footwork · 1v1 spreading.

**Fun / warm-up games**
- Rondo · sharks & minnows · king of the ring · tag/possession hybrids · "traffic" dribbling · relays. (Engagement and volume of touches for young players.)

## Choosing a method (rules of thumb)

| You want to… | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Groove a brand-new technique | isolated/block practice, high reps |
| Build decision-making & transfer | SSG, game-based, random practice |
| Fix a specific team pattern | phase of play / pattern play |
| Develop adaptable, robust skill | constraints-led, differential learning |
| Maximize touches & fun (young kids) | SSG, rondos, dribbling games |
| Train a moment (turnover, press) | transition & conditioned games |

> **Curriculum note:** For the golden age, bias toward **games** and **volume of
> touches** over queues and lines. A child in a 4v4 gets ~5× the ball contacts of
> one in an 11v11 drill. Keep everyone active, everyone with a ball where possible.
