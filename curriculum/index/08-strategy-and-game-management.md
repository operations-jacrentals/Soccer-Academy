# 08 · Strategy & Game Management

If tactics are *how you play*, strategy is *what you decide* — before, during, and
across matches and seasons. The thinking layer that adapts tactics to opponent,
scoreline, and long-term aims.

---

## Pre-match strategy

- **Game plan / game model** — the intended way to play this match, from your identity + the opponent.
- **Opposition analysis (scouting)** — strengths, weaknesses, patterns, key players, set-piece habits.
- **Matching up** — choosing a shape/approach vs. their formation (e.g., matching their front line, exploiting their weak side).
- **Exploiting weaknesses** — targeting a slow full-back, a high line, poor aerial defending, a keeper weak on crosses.
- **Contingency planning** — pre-agreed "if X, then Y" adjustments.

## In-game management

- **Game states** — behavior changes when **chasing** (need a goal), **protecting a lead**, or **level**. Risk appetite shifts with the scoreline and clock.
- **In-game adjustments** — changing shape, pressing height, or match-ups mid-game to solve problems.
- **Substitutions** — for impact, energy, game-state, or to change the shape; timing and message.
- **Tempo control** — speeding up (press, quick restarts) or slowing down (keep-ball, deliberate build).
- **Momentum management** — riding a good spell; halting the opponent's (a foul, a sub, a slow restart, a shape change).
- **Killing the game / game management** — seeing out a result: keeping possession, smart fouls, using the corners, managing restarts.
- **Time management & the dark arts** — (professionally) slowing restarts, tactical fouls to stop counters, gamesmanship — taught with a strong ethics frame in a youth setting.
- **Managing 10 men** — reshaping after a red card to stay compact and dangerous.
- **Half-time adjustments** — the structured reset: what's working, what to change, clear messages.
- **Penalty shootout strategy** — order, roles, keeper research, mentality (see [05](05-psychology-and-mental.md)).

## Risk management

- **Where to take risks** — high-value zones (final third) vs. safety-first zones (own box).
- **Risk vs. reward by area** — playing out under press (reward: control; risk: conceding) vs. going long.
- **Protecting against the counter** — rest defense and controlled aggression.

## Season & program strategy

- **Playing identity / "DNA"** — the consistent style the club/academy commits to (e.g., possession + high press). Anchors every decision.
- **Squad rotation & peaking** — spreading load, targeting key fixtures/tournaments.
- **Cup vs. league approach** — one-off knockout thinking vs. long campaign consistency.
- **Development vs. results balance** — in youth, *development wins first*: minutes, position rotation, and learning over trophies.
- **Recruitment & pathway strategy** — how players enter, progress, and move up (see [11](11-player-development-and-pathways.md)).
- **Long-term periodized planning** — sequencing themes across a season/multi-year plan (the Academy's 5-year arc).

## Strategic frameworks (thinking tools)

- **Strengths vs. weaknesses (yours & theirs)** — pit your strengths at their weaknesses; protect your weaknesses from their strengths.
- **The four moments lens** — plan explicitly for each (see [07](07-tactics-and-systems.md)).
- **Marginal gains** — many small edges (set pieces, restarts, fitness, recovery) compounding.
- **Decision hierarchy** — safety in your third, control in the middle third, risk/creativity in the final third.

> **Curriculum note:** Strategy is a *thinking skill* — grow it with questions,
> not commands. Ask young players "what's the score, how long left, what should we
> do?" Debrief decisions after games. Keep the "dark arts" firmly inside a
> sportsmanship and ethics conversation — this is where the Academy's character
> mission and the game meet.
