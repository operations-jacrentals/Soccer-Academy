# 12 · Laws of the Game & Youth Formats

Rules literacy is part of a soccer education — players who understand the laws make
better decisions. This also covers the **age-appropriate game formats** that make
development work at each stage.

---

## The 17 Laws of the Game (IFAB)

1. **The Field of Play** — dimensions, markings, goals.
2. **The Ball** — size, material, pressure.
3. **The Players** — number (11), substitutions, sending-off effects.
4. **The Players' Equipment** — kit, shin guards, safety (no jewelry).
5. **The Referee** — authority, powers, duties.
6. **The Other Match Officials** — assistants, fourth official, VAR.
7. **The Duration of the Match** — halves, added time, half-time.
8. **The Start and Restart of Play** — kick-off, dropped ball.
9. **The Ball In and Out of Play** — the whole ball over the line.
10. **Determining the Outcome of a Match** — goals, winner, tie-breaks.
11. **Offside** — position + involvement; the game's most-debated law.
12. **Fouls and Misconduct** — direct/indirect free-kick offenses, cards, handball.
13. **Free Kicks** — direct vs. indirect, wall distance **9.15 m / 10 yд** ✅ ([IFAB Law 13](../references/sources.md)).
14. **The Penalty Kick** — award, procedure, encroachment.
15. **The Throw-In** — technique, restart from the touchline.
16. **The Goal Kick** — restart from the goal area.
17. **The Corner Kick** — restart from the corner arc.

## Key concepts to teach explicitly

- **Offside** — level is onside; involvement matters (interfering with play/opponent, gaining advantage). Teach attackers timing runs and defenders the line/trap.
- **Handball** — deliberate vs. natural silhouette; the law evolves — keep current.
- **Advantage** — referee lets play continue when stopping would help the offending team.
- **Cards & discipline** — yellow (caution) / red (send-off); accumulation; the "professional foul."
- **VAR** — for the four reviewable categories (goals, penalties, red cards, mistaken identity) — relevant to understanding the modern pro game.
- **Fair play & sportsmanship** — the *spirit* of the laws (ties to [08](08-strategy-and-game-management.md) and the character mission).

## Youth game formats (development-appropriate)

Smaller formats = more touches, more decisions, more involvement, faster learning.

| Age (approx) | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| U6–U8 | **4v4** (no GK or small goals) | maximum touches, everyone involved, no hiding |
| U9–U10 | **7v7** (with GK) | introduces GK, wide play, basic shape |
| U11–U12 | **9v9** | bridges to the full game; more positions and space |
| U13+ | **11v11** | the full game once players can handle the space |

**Ball & field sizing**
- **Size 3** ball (~U8 and below) · **Size 4** (~U9–U12) · **Size 5** (U13+/adult). ✅ Formats and ball sizes per [US Soccer PDI 2017](../references/sources.md); full-size ball spec per [IFAB Law 2](../references/sources.md).
- Field, goal, and penalty-area dimensions scale down for younger ages.

**Other youth modifications**
- **Equal playing time** and **position rotation** — everyone plays, everywhere.
- **Retreat lines / build-out lines** — encourage playing out from the back, discourage "boot it long."
- **Heading guidance (US Soccer, 2016)** ✅ — **no deliberate heading at U11 and younger** (practices *or* games); **U12–U13 limited** to ≤30 min/week of heading training (~15–20 headers/player/week), no in-game restriction. A player *playing up* follows the **older** group's rule (a 10-year-old in a U12 group doesn't head). Adopt the protective standard ([Doc Standard](../DOC_STANDARD.md) Rule 2). → [US Soccer heading guidelines](../references/sources.md#us-soccer-heading-guidelines-2016)
- **Development over results** — de-emphasized league tables at young ages; some bodies don't publish standings for the youngest.
- **Bio-banding events** — grouping by maturity for fairer, richer competition (see [11](11-player-development-and-pathways.md)).

> **Curriculum note:** Pick formats by **stage, not just age** — a small-sided,
> retreat-line, position-rotating environment does more for development than
> pushing kids into 11v11 early. Teach the laws little-and-often through the games
> themselves (a quick "why was that offside?") rather than as rulebook lectures.
